# 2027 Mock Weekly Plan

A first-pass outline of the 12-week series starting January 2027. Dates are
blank until the schedule is set. Project formats come from
[project-classification.md](project-classification.md); open questions are in
[interview-questions.md](interview-questions.md).

## Shape of the series

- **Weeks 1-3: understand and choose.** Meet the partners, research the
  problems, pitch an approach. This puts the business focus early, as the 2026
  retro asked.
- **Weeks 4-7: learn and build.** Three class workshops deliver small partner
  asks while teaching skills the longer projects reuse.
- **Weeks 8-9: test and adjust.** Partners see the work in progress.
- **Weeks 10-12: polish and present.** Project sprint, then Student Demo Day.

Every session is 2 hours: a 30-40 minute skill segment, then hands-on time.
Students are on their own project from week 3 ("project from the beginning"
in the retro).

## Week by week

### Week 0: Parent prep night (optional)

- **Goal:** families arrive at week 1 with working laptops and accounts.
- **Activity:** pizza, account setup, walk through the series and Demo Day
  date, explain that students work with real community partners.
- **Prep:** setup guide (`SETUP.md`), consent and photo release forms, a list
  of which accounts have age restrictions.

### Week 1: Kickoff: meet the partners

- **Goal:** students know the problems on offer and who has them.
- **Skill segment:** human-centered design: who is the user, what do they
  need, how would we know we helped?
- **Hands-on:** partner lightning talks (live or recorded, 3 minutes each),
  then students write "who, problem, why it matters" cards for the projects
  that interest them.
- **Students leave with:** a ranked list of their top three projects.
- **Prep:** partner interviews finished; a one-page summary per project;
  partners invited or recorded.

### Week 2: Understand the problem

- **Goal:** students can describe the problem without describing a solution.
- **Skill segment:** interviewing users and researching what already exists,
  including ideas that succeeded and failed (retro request).
- **Hands-on:** in provisional teams, write interview questions for the
  partner and find two existing solutions to compare.
- **Students leave with:** a project assignment and a question list for their
  partner.
- **Prep:** project assignments drafted from week 1 rankings; a partner
  contact method that works for minors (mentor on every message).

### Week 3: Pitch competition

- **Goal:** each project has a clear, small, agreed scope.
- **Skill segment:** refining an idea and pitching it in 2 minutes.
- **Hands-on:** teams draft and deliver a pitch: user, problem, proposed
  solution, what "done" looks like at Demo Day. Friendly judging with
  feedback rather than elimination, since every project continues.
- **Students leave with:** a one-page project brief.
- **Prep:** pitch template, judges, a brief template with a "student fills
  in" section.

### Week 4: Prototyping and design, with the trail sign workshop

- **Goal:** students sketch before they build.
- **Skill segment:** design sketches, paper prototypes, AI-assisted mockups.
- **Class workshop:** every student designs a sign for Friends of the Lansing
  Regional Trail from the partner's brief. The partner picks favorites.
- **Hands-on (project):** sketch or prototype your own project's main screen,
  storyboard, or layout.
- **Students leave with:** a sign design and a project prototype.
- **Prep:** sign dimensions, required content, brand rules, and example
  signs from the partner.

### Week 5: Forms and inventories workshop

- **Goal:** students can turn a manual list into a structured, shareable one.
- **Skill segment:** structuring data, form design, and handling personal
  information responsibly.
- **Class workshop:** split the class three ways on the same pattern: Advent
  House needs list and meal signup, Costume Giveaway inventory, and a Haven
  House housing form prototype using made-up data only.
- **Hands-on (project):** set up the project workspace and get one piece
  working.
- **Students leave with:** a working form or inventory and a project
  workspace.
- **Prep:** sample data from each partner with personal details removed; a
  decision on tools (see the series-level questions).

### Week 6: Building with AI responsibly

- **Goal:** students use AI tools deliberately on their own project.
- **Skill segment:** human-centered AI: when AI helps the user, when it
  harms, and how to check its work. Reuses the AI Builder series habits (one
  thing at a time, save points).
- **Hands-on:** build the project's core feature.
- **Students leave with:** the core feature working, saved in version
  control.
- **Prep:** confirm AI tool access and token limits per student (retro
  dislike); have a fallback for students who are locked out.

### Week 7: Telling a story with video

- **Goal:** students can cut photos and clips into a short story.
- **Skill segment:** story arc, pacing, titles, music and licensing.
- **Class workshop:** build the Essential Blessings 2026 events video from
  their photos. Small groups each take one event, then the cuts are joined.
- **Hands-on (project):** midpoint demo to the class, 2 minutes each.
- **Students leave with:** a video segment and peer feedback on their
  project.
- **Prep:** photos sorted by event, permission to publish, a chosen editing
  tool that runs on every student laptop.

### Week 8: Partner check-in

- **Goal:** partners see real work while there is still time to change it.
- **Skill segment:** asking for feedback and deciding what to cut ("cut scope
  before cutting confidence").
- **Hands-on:** show the partner (in person, by video call, or by recorded
  demo), collect feedback, re-plan the remaining weeks.
- **Students leave with:** a prioritized feedback list and a trimmed scope.
- **Prep:** partner availability confirmed in advance; a feedback form.

### Week 9: Build and test with real users

- **Goal:** the project works for someone who did not build it.
- **Skill segment:** watching someone use your project without helping them.
- **Hands-on:** swap projects with another team, record what confused them,
  fix the top issues.
- **Students leave with:** a feature-complete project and a bug list.
- **Prep:** test script template.

### Week 10: Project sprint 1

- **Goal:** polish and fix.
- **Hands-on:** bug fixes, finishing touches, start the demo recording.
- **Reuse:** `project-sprint/index.md`.

### Week 11: Project sprint 2

- **Goal:** ready to present and ready to hand off.
- **Hands-on:** Demo Day submission form, slides, rehearsal, and a handoff
  note for the partner (what it is, how to use it, how to update it, who to
  ask).
- **Reuse:** `project-sprint/week-2.md` and `project-sprint/week-3.md`.

### Week 12: Student Demo Day

- **Goal:** celebrate and hand over.
- **Activity:** presentations to families and partners, partner handoff,
  student and partner feedback forms.
- **Reuse:** `student-demo-day-2026/` program and slides.
- **Prep:** venue, invitations to partners, feedback forms.

## Things this plan assumes

- Demo Day is session 12, which leaves two sprint weeks instead of 2026's
  three. If Demo Day is a 13th session, add a sprint week.
- Three class workshops is the right balance. Each one costs project time, so
  dropping one is the first lever if projects fall behind.
- The new volunteers are available in weeks 1, 3, 4, and 6.
- Running documentation (progress, challenges, wins) is a 5-minute journal
  entry at the end of each session rather than a separate activity.
