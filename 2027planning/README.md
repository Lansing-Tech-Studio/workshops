# 2027 Planning

This folder is for brainstorming around the tech workshops starting in January of 2027. For this set of workshops, we are providing students with opportunities to work on real-world problems, providing solutions for the community. The list of projects is in the projectlist.md file. For individual projects, they could be something that is handled by one or more students working fairly independently. Or, for simple enough projects where it's a good learning opportunity, we can do it as an individual workshop, where, for the 2-hour block, we as a class create the solution. 

We aim to balance between independent projects and collaborative workshops to maximize both learning and community impact.

We will also consider the skill levels and interests of the students when assigning projects, ensuring that each student has the opportunity to contribute meaningfully while also learning new skills.

Additionally, we will maintain open communication with the community partners to ensure that the solutions we develop align with their needs and expectations, fostering a collaborative and impactful learning environment.

We have some new volunteers we're trying to bring in for mentoring, perhaps leading workshops. One person has experience with advertising, and they were talking to us about having a pitch competition to show kids how to refine an idea. There was another person who wants to focus on human-centered AI, and that person has a background in user experience. Additionally, we have Adam, who taught some business and marketing research concepts last year, and Joe Dearman, who introduced the kids to some prototyping, design sketches, and AI modeling. I believe that these are the skills that make sense earlier in our 12-week series.

I target for the end of the workshop series will be once again to have a Student Demo Day, where students can showcase the projects they have worked on, demonstrating their learning and the impact of their solutions on the community. This event will serve as a culmination of the students' efforts and a celebration of their achievements, providing an opportunity for community members to engage with and support the students' work.

Throughout the workshop series, we will also document the students' progress, challenges, and successes, creating a record that can be used for reflection, assessment, and future planning. This documentation will help us continuously improve the workshop experience and ensure that we are meeting our educational and community goals.

Finally, we will seek feedback from both students and community partners to understand the effectiveness of our workshops and identify areas for improvement. This feedback loop will be crucial in refining our approach and ensuring that our workshops remain relevant, engaging, and impactful for all participants.
