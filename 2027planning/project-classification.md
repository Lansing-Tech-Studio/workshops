# 2027 Project Classification

Draft sort of every idea in [projectlist.md](projectlist.md) into a format.
Everything here is a starting guess to be corrected after partner interviews
(see [interview-questions.md](interview-questions.md)).

## Formats

- **Class workshop:** the whole class builds it together in one 2-hour session.
  Fits small, well-bounded asks that teach a skill other projects reuse.
- **Group project:** 2-4 students, weeks 3-12. Fits asks that are too big for
  one person and have separable roles (content, design, build, partner contact).
- **Individual or pair project:** one clear owner, weeks 3-12. Fits
  self-contained asks where scope can flex with skill level.
- **Not a build project:** an opportunity to schedule rather than something to
  make.

## Class workshops

### 1. Trail sign graphic design

- **Why this format:** every student can produce a finished design in one
  session, and the partner gets many options to choose from.
- **Skills:** layout, type, contrast, designing for print and for outdoors.
- **Pairs with:** week 4 prototyping and design sketches.
- **Biggest unknown:** sign dimensions, required content, and brand rules.
- **Risk:** the partner may want a single consistent sign family, which means
  one student or a small group finishes the chosen design afterward.

### 2. Advent House needs inventory and meal signup

- **Why this format:** a shared spreadsheet plus a public view and a signup
  form is a pattern the class can build in one sitting.
- **Skills:** structuring data, forms, publishing a read-only view.
- **Biggest unknown:** who updates the list day to day, and with what tool.
- **Risk:** meal signup collects volunteer names and contact details. The
  partner must own the live form and the responses.
- **Could grow into:** a group project if they want it embedded in their own
  website with reminders.

### 3. Costume Giveaway inventory list

- **Why this format:** same pattern as Advent House, so the class builds both
  from one lesson and compares them.
- **Skills:** categories, sizes, counts, filtering.
- **Biggest unknown:** whether the list is public (families browse it) or
  internal (volunteers track stock).

### 4. Haven House housing form

- **Why this format:** form design is a one-session skill and a strong lesson
  in writing clear questions for stressed readers.
- **Skills:** plain-language questions, required versus optional fields,
  accessibility, privacy.
- **Biggest unknown:** what happens to a submission after it is sent.
- **Risk:** this is the most sensitive item on the list. People applying for
  housing share personal details. Students build a prototype with made-up
  data; Haven House staff create and own the real form. Students never see
  real submissions.

### 5. Essential Blessings 2026 events video

- **Why this format:** the photos already exist, so a session on pacing,
  titles, and music produces a finished video.
- **Skills:** storytelling, sequencing, captions, music licensing.
- **Pairs with:** the Lansing Tech Studio video team, who can use it as
  practice.
- **Biggest unknown:** photo count and quality, and permission to show the
  people in them.
- **Could grow into:** an individual project if the class version needs a
  polish pass.

## Group projects

### 6. Trail forms and invoice automation

- **Why this format:** it is three linked jobs (sign inventory, sponsorship
  agreements, annual invoices), each of which one student can own.
- **Skills:** spreadsheets as a database, document templates and mail merge,
  scheduled reminders, writing handoff instructions.
- **Biggest unknown:** which tools the partner can keep using after Demo Day
  (they are on Excel and Word today).
- **Risk:** highest maintenance burden on the list. Sponsor records and
  invoices are real money and real contacts. Aim for a low-code solution the
  partner's volunteers can run without us.
- **Suggested size:** 3 students.

### 7. Special Needs resource website

- **Why this format:** splits naturally into content gathering, site
  structure, and design.
- **Skills:** information architecture, accessibility, writing for families.
- **Biggest unknown:** who the partner is, and who keeps the resource list
  current.
- **Risk:** a resource directory goes stale fast. It needs a named owner
  before students start.
- **Suggested size:** 3-4 students.

### 8 and 9. Lansing Tech Studio promo video and building transformation video

- **Why this format:** one video team with roles (script, camera, edit,
  sound) can make both; we are our own partner, so feedback is fast.
- **Skills:** scripting, interviewing, shooting, editing, sound design (the
  2026 retro asked for more sound and foley work).
- **Biggest unknown:** whether before-and-during footage of the building
  exists.
- **Suggested size:** 3-4 students.

### 10. MSU farm or garden robot

- **Why this format:** hardware needs several hands, and the 2026 retro asked
  for more hardware.
- **Skills:** depends entirely on the task.
- **Biggest unknown:** everything: which site, what the robot does, budget,
  access to the site, and safety rules.
- **Risk:** least defined and most expensive item. Marked as a stretch
  project. If the interview does not produce a concrete task, offer it as a
  research and prototype project (a design plus a tabletop model) instead of a
  working robot.
- **Suggested size:** 3-4 students with a dedicated mentor.

## Individual or pair projects

### 11. Mitten Raised Bakery game or cake decorating app

- **Why this format:** self-contained, fun, and close to the game work
  students liked most in 2026.
- **Skills:** web or game engine development, drag and drop, art assets.
- **Biggest unknown:** whether "game" and "cake decorating app" are one thing
  or two, and where customers would use it.

### 12. Costume Giveaway game

- **Why this format:** same as the bakery game.
- **Biggest unknown:** the purpose: entertain kids waiting in line, promote
  the event, or something else.

### 13. FIRST competition schedule app

- **Why this format:** one clear feature set with a real data source.
- **Skills:** working with an API or published schedule, lists and filters,
  mobile layout.
- **Biggest unknown:** which FIRST program and whether existing apps already
  cover the need (the list itself says "maybe").

### 14. Lansing First Nazarene website updates

- **Why this format:** a few pages of content on an existing site.
- **Skills:** writing clear web content, working inside someone else's site.
- **Biggest unknown:** what platform the site runs on and whether a student
  can be given edit access.
- **Fallback:** if access is not possible, the student delivers the page
  content and a mockup for church staff to publish.

### 15. Capital Area Christian Co-op website updates

- **Why this format:** same as Lansing First Nazarene.
- **Biggest unknown:** what the updates are. The ask has no detail yet.

## Not a build project

### 16. Lansing Arena shadow days

Ages 14+ only, so it does not fit a class of mixed ages. Treat it as an
optional field experience scheduled outside the 12 sessions, and ask whether
younger students can take a tour instead.

## Load check

If every non-workshop project runs, that is 4 group projects and 5 individual
or pair projects: 9 projects needing roughly 18-25 students and enough mentors
to cover them. Likely more than one cohort can carry, so the interviews should
also establish which partners can wait until a later series.
